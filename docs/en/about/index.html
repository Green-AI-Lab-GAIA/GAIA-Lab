About Us

The laboratory develops artificial intelligence applied to geospatial and Earth observation data, focusing on learning useful representations of complex planetary events. The central research question is how environmental systems interact with each other, investigating how climate, forests, agriculture, and cities influence each other in cascading ways, and how this coupling varies across regions.

In parallel with orbital observation, the laboratory develops and applies language models to technical, institutional, and regulatory documents, supporting policy evaluation and governance.

Our history

  1. 2022

    Founding at FGV EMAp

    Launch of Green AI Lab activities at FGV EMAp, in Rio de Janeiro, in September, dedicated to developing machine learning methods for Earth observation and analysis of scarce ground truth data.

  2. 2023

    Foundation models and research consolidation

    Professor Dario Oliveira's co-authorship in developing Prithvi, an open-source geospatial foundation model arising from his collaboration at IBM Research alongside NASA, brought a rich exchange of experience and mindset. This background inspired an internal shift in perspective, fostering the consolidation of the laboratory and the structuring of research lines.

  3. 2025

    14th Climate Informatics Conference (CI2025)

    Hosted for the first time in the Global South at the FGV Cultural Center in Rio de Janeiro, this international conference was co-organized by FGV EMAp and IBM Research Brazil. The laboratory took an active role in organization, scientific presentations, and connecting students directly with the global climate informatics community.

  4. 2026

    Project expansion and natural language processing

    The laboratory's projects achieve broad coverage across entire states and the country, driven by new research partnerships and the launch of new investigative avenues such as natural language processing.

How we work

01
Replicable science

Open methods and auditable code. Only stable results proven beyond statistical noise margins are validated.

02
Territorial auditing

Models evaluated against INPE monitoring programs (DETER and PRODES), agricultural risk zoning (ZARC), and land use references such as MapBiomas.

03
Territorial scale

Pipelines engineered for nationwide execution, processing terabytes of satellite data across multi-year time series.

04
Limits and uncertainties

Explicit mapping of where models fail or lose sensitivity, never hiding limitations behind aggregated averages.

05
Weekly follow-ups

Weekly meetings to evaluate progress and a shared digital board for continuous guidance of each research line.

06
Methodological synergy

Continuous integration between methodological research and direct application to real-world challenges, working alongside domain specialists in agriculture, forestry, and climate.

Research infrastructure and environment

Institutional partnerships

The laboratory collaborates closely with Fundação Getulio Vargas research centers and civil society organizations to connect data science methods with real-world challenges.

01
COIAB

Coordination of Indigenous Organizations of the Brazilian Amazon. Joint work on territorial monitoring tools and the protection of Indigenous lands across the Amazon.

02
FGV Agro

Center for Agribusiness Studies. Territorial-scale crop parcel mapping and predictive models for harvest tracking and sustainable rural credit.

03
FGV CES

Center for Sustainability Studies. Sustainability indicators, ecological integrity metrics, and socio-environmental policy research.

04
FGV CERI

Center for Regulation and Infrastructure Studies. Sustainable infrastructure, energy transition, and regulation of environmental assets.

05
FGV Projetos

Technical advisory unit. Applying analytical solutions to sustainable development projects and public policy initiatives.

06
FGV PMO

FGV Project Management Office. Institutional coordination, governance for research initiatives, and support for strategic partnership programs.
